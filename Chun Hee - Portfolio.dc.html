The Product — UI/UX I Designed

Gacha, screen by screen.

The live game at gacha.thechillgallery.com. I designed its screens and flows and prototyped them with Claude Code — engineering adopted the prototype as the working spec.

Screens & flows: mine · Mark, pack & card art: the brand's

The pack screen, redrawn.

The screen the whole product turns on — if opening a pack does not feel like an event here, nothing later can rescue it. This is what it was, and what it became.

Beforethe shipped build45% of the screen doing nothing

One pack, one button, one collapsed row of numbers — and beneath it, most of a screen holding nothing at all.

Afterlive nowevery pixel given a job

The refills became a ring you can see and touch. The empty half became the panel that governs it.

Four decisions, and what each one prevents.

Refills became a ring

A number in a collapsed strip — Refills: 0/10 — that you read rather than used.

A count is something you check. Ten bolts around the pack are something you spend. Tapping one returns a pack, so the resource and the action are the same object.

The dead half became the panel

Content stopped just under halfway down. The rest was black.

A hero screen that ends in nothing reads as unfinished, and it trains the player not to look down. Energy, boosts and both spend actions now live in that space.

One action became two

A single Open Pack (Free).

Use Refills (1) and Use all Refills (10) are for two different people — the one savouring a pull and the one clearing a stack before work.

The pack got its weight back

Small, flat, adrift in the middle of the frame.

It is the product. I staged the brand's art at roughly twice the area, with rim light and depth, so it is the largest thing on the screen.

Before the account.

There is no “before” for these — the product opened on a sign-in box and nothing else. Between them they make the whole case: let them hold a pack before asking them for anything.

01

Nothing is asked for first

A pack in an empty hall, one line under it — guaranteed one of each rarity — and Tap to open. No email, no account, no wallet. The demo pack is the argument, and it is made before the ask rather than behind it.

02

And when it is, the product is still playing

The auth card sits inside the product rather than in front of it. Email or Telegram, nothing else — a Telegram-first product saying so by what it leaves out.
